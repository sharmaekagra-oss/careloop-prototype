The Petrol-Pump Lockout

One afternoon, one bike, one queue, one SIM — and what breaks when the world's largest real-time payment system meets the world's most fragile last mile.

Ekagra Sharma · sharma.ekagra@gmail.com · MICA PLM:ES · Sep 2026

What this is

An incident log, then an argument. I am the subject and the researcher. Autoethnography is a qualitative method in which the researcher's own experience is treated as data and examined against external evidence.1 What follows is one afternoon. Timestamps are approximate; the sequence is not.

The log

15:40Tank empties on the flyover approaching the petrol pump. Bike coasts.
15:44I get off and push. The pedestrian crossing on the flyover intersection does not exist. Cars route around me. I keep walking.
15:52Pump. Attendant nods. Fill ₹200.
15:55Open GPay. QR scan. "Cannot connect."
15:56Signal indicator: "No SIM." Mid-transaction. This is not the network. This is the phone.
15:57Queue behind me: three deep. Attendant looks at me. I look at the attendant.
15:58Ask the man behind for a hotspot. He gives it. VoIP call to a friend.
16:01Friend pays ₹200 to the attendant on his own UPI. I owe him. Queue moves on.
16:20Mobile repair shop. "CPU problem. ₹3,000. Two days."
16:22I borrow an ejector pin. Swap SIM slots. Signal returns. Cost: zero.

The five failed fallbacks

Between 15:56 and 16:01, I ran through my mental map of alternatives. Each one had already lost:

  1. Cash. I didn't have any. I don't carry any.
  2. Debit card. I don't own one that I use day-to-day.
  3. UPI Lite. I hadn't loaded it. I barely knew it existed.
  4. UPI 123Pay. I didn't know what it was.
  5. A stranger. The one that worked. Not a product. A social act.

What this document is about

Four problems interlock in this one incident: pedestrian infrastructure, mid-transaction payment lockout, repair-shop overquoting, and my own single-rail dependency. Three of them belong to other people's specialties. The fourth — the payment lockout — is the structural one. India built the world's largest real-time payments system on top of one SIM, one signal, one phone.2 This is what happens when that stack fails, and what happens next is what the rest of this document is about.

Page 2 · The Job, The Pain, The Wrong Answer

Target customer

The young urban Indian who has stopped carrying cash. Concentrated in metros and Tier-2 cities. One smartphone, one SIM (increasingly eSIM), one bank-linked UPI. Uses GPay or PhonePe as primary; Paytm as backup on the same rails. Estimated ~30–50 million at the sharpest edge (tech workers, students, urban professionals whose default wallet contains no cash), inside a broader UPI-primary segment of ~200–300 million within India's 500M+ unique UPI users.3 This is not the average Indian. It is a specific cohort — the one that has run furthest ahead of cash.

Jobs-to-be-Done — Session 3, Abhay Jani: situation → motivation → outcome

  • Functional. When my primary payment rail (SIM → data → UPI) fails at the point of transaction, I want to complete the transaction anyway, so I can finish what I was doing without stopping.
  • Emotional. When my rail fails in front of a queue, I want to not feel powerless over my own money, so I can stop the moment from becoming the memory of the transaction. (A netizen quoted in Deccan Herald put it precisely: "no control over our own money."4)
  • Social. When the rail fails in public, I want to not have to ask a stranger for a hotspot or beg a friend on WhatsApp, so I can stop the transaction from becoming a favour I now owe.

Non-consumption observation — Session 3, strong signal

"Aariz Ahmed said he was frustrated to have to get down mid-journey just because he didn't have cash, as he usually never carries cash and solely depends on digital payments." — The Hindu-Bengaluru, 11 May 2024, reporting on the BMTC airport-bus UPI outage.6

A named commuter, in a mainstream Indian daily, confessing to non-consumption of cash. He is the target user. His cohort is growing.

Killed hypothesis — Session 4, the research-over-assumptions test

Before I did the ethnography, my hypothesis was "this is a repair-shop-trust problem" — anchored to the ejector-pin moment (₹3,000 quote for a fix that cost me nothing). I was going to write about mechanic overquoting.

Two pieces of evidence killed it:

  1. The digital ethnography (page 3) surfaced repeating, named, dated payment-lockout stories from mainstream Indian press across 2024–2026. Repair overquotes are anecdotal. Payment lockouts are structural.
  2. The regulator is fixing the payment problem, not the repair problem. RBI and NPCI have both publicly named this, and are building rails for it (page 4). When the regulator agrees with your problem, you have picked the right problem.

The repair-shop story survives as page 1's ejector-pin punchline. It is where I began. It is not what I ended up with.

Evidence — named, dated, cited

  1. The Bangalore BMTC bus, 10–11 May 2024. Two-day UPI failure on BMTC's Canara-Bank-backed ETMs on airport routes. Two named commuters quoted in The Hindu-Bengaluru: Rajesh Kumar (forced to disembark and hail an auto because he had no cash) and Aariz Ahmed (the non-consumption quote above).
  2. The teamblind stranded-at-petrol-pump post. A tech worker in India describes being stuck at a petrol pump with no cash, no card, and UPI down after both GPay and Paytm failed. Same demographic as this document's target user. Thread research/reddit_quotes.md.7
  3. The three UPI outages of Mar–Apr 2025. 26 Mar → 2 Apr → 12 Apr. The 12 Apr outage was the longest in years (~5 hours) and affected GPay, PhonePe, and Paytm simultaneously. Root cause per NPCI's RCA: PSP banks flooding "check transaction status" APIs at unsustainable TPS. Deccan Herald aggregated netizen anecdotes: users joking about "cleaned vessels at the restaurant" to work off unpayable bills, and comparing themselves to the RBI Governor with "no control over our own money."8
  4. Airtel, 24 Aug 2025. 6,800+ Downdetector complaints at peak. 52% "No Signal," 31% mobile-data failure. Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Chennai, Kolkata.9
  5. My own incident, page 1. Different failure mode — SIM hardware, not network — same outcome.

Delta 4 verdict. The de-facto workaround (~3/10) sets a low bar. Cash (~7/10) works but the cohort has decoupled from it. UPI Lite as it exists (~6/10) is the closest live product — and its Delta over the workaround is only ~3, which explains why it hasn't taken over as the mental default despite being live since 2022. A well-designed offline-first payment credential should be able to hit 8–9/10 — clearing Delta 4 with room to spare.

Page 4 · The Opportunity

Product opportunity

A pre-authorised, on-device offline payment credential that spends without SIM, signal, or data at the moment of transaction. Small pre-loaded balance (cap ~₹2,000, auto-refill when connectivity returns), NFC tap-to-pay to any merchant PoS, cryptographic settlement, PIN-optional for micro-payments. Cross-bank by design. Aimed squarely at "the young urban Indian who has stopped carrying cash and now cannot pay because their SIM died." Distributed pre-emptively — bundled into a super-app or wallet, or as an OS-level integration, not sold in the moment (users don't shop for backup payments while stranded).

Why now — specific, dated, three convergent shifts

  1. The regulator publicly agrees. RBI raised UPI Lite per-txn to ₹1,000 and wallet cap to ₹5,000 in Dec 2024, and updated the offline-payments framework. NPCI is certifying NFC PoS terminals from major manufacturers through 2026; UPI Lite X rollout targeted end-2026. HDFC OfflinePay has RBI approval. The rails are being built.
  2. The pain is repeated and mainstream-covered. Three UPI outages in 17 days (Mar–Apr 2025), one of them ~5 hours across all major apps. Airtel Aug 2025 outage: 6,800+ complaints peak. Jio Sep 2024. Airtel Feb 2025 & Jun 2026. The outage narrative gets louder every year.
  3. The cohort has inflected. Cash paradox (RBI Deputy Governor Murmu): digital payments are additive, not substitutive — absolute cash grows but a decoupled-from-cash cohort has emerged and is the fastest-growing user segment. Aariz Ahmed's quote is the leading edge, not the exception.

Any one of these is a plausible "why now." All three converging is the argument.

Willingness-to-pay & market size — honest ranges

  • Serviceable market: 30–50M sharpest-edge users (young urban decoupled-from-cash) × payment-fallback events per year. Even at 5 events/user/year that is 150–250M annual "lockout moments."
  • Monetisation shapes: per-transaction interchange (regulated), premium wallet tier, or B2B distribution (bundled with a phone maker, a bank, a fintech). Direct-to-consumer WTP is thin (users won't shop for this). Distribution-side WTP is real — a bank or phone maker will pay for a differentiated "always-pays" feature.
  • Painkiller vs vitamin (Session 5): painkiller. Composite pain 12/15; social visibility puts it in the "I would pay right now" bracket, even if retrospective WTP is lower.
Go / No-Go: GO, with two risks disclosed.

Risk 1 — NPCI is building this.

UPI Lite X + NFC PoS certification lands on the exact scope described above. A neutral startup opportunity therefore cannot be a fresh payment rail. It can be an experience and adoption layer — invisible-by-default, cross-bank, pre-authorised, bundled into distribution channels users already use. This is a design + distribution play, not a payment-rail play.

Risk 2 — Distribution is pre-emptive, not in-the-moment.

Users don't shop for offline payments while stranded. Distribution has to piggyback on OEM pre-installs, bank-onboarding flows, or super-app defaults. If either risk cannot be de-risked in the next 12 weeks of primary research (5 stakeholder conversations with a bank, an OEM, and NPCI-adjacent product folks; a paper prototype tested with 15 users in the same cohort), revise to No-Go and revisit.
